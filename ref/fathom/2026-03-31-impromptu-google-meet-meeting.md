### Job margin philosophy: splits vs. logical pricing 0:04

- TruSpark stopped using a fixed 60/40 split with contractors; instead he prices each job to what the market will bear, often ending up ahead of a flat split anyway.
- Pushing prices too high on a percentage split burns deals — customers who feel gouged just walk, so pricing has to stay "logical" for them.

### Service call fees: what to charge 2:38

- Devin asked the group about service/inspection fees; most plumbers he'd talked to said $60 (waivable) was too low, and recommended closer to $100.
- Eric argued a plumber complaining about a $60-100 fee probably has too much business already; a plumber short on work should be willing to pay to quote a job.
- Art charges $39 per inspection; TruSpark moved away from flat inspection fees entirely — he takes a credit card on file (no charge) to confirm seriousness, then charges $150-250 for the actual diagnostic/inspection visit, waived if the customer proceeds with the work.
- TruSpark's reasoning: charging too little reads as low-quality ("if someone's charging so little... they must not be good enough"); charging a real number and staying transparent about "price starts at X" builds trust and filters out non-serious leads.

### Business registration and taxes 12:10

### On-page vs. off-page SEO approaches 29:40

- Art leans heavily on off-page SEO: backlinks, directory listings/citations, and (relative to Eric) more website-level SEO work.
- Eric's edge is in acquiring real business addresses, which Art called a "very big play" but difficult to get.
- A strong central website that all location pages/GMBs link back to gives every new GMB profile an immediate SEO boost, even with zero reviews — versus a weak or absent website, which leaves each new GMB starting from zero. The website itself feeds Google ranking signals for the linked GMB profiles.
- Multi-location setups: one website hosting per-city landing pages, each linked to its corresponding GMB listing.

### Service-area vs. address-based profiles 32:22

- Devin's informal testing found service-area profiles can outrank address-based ones if the service-area profile has enough reviews.
- Art disagreed with that conclusion in general — address-based profiles are proven to outrank service-area profiles by default; more reviews can offset that but not everyone can generate 2x reviews.
- Given Devin's specific edge at generating reviews fast, and that service-area profiles are easier to spin up (new one per day), Art agreed Devin's competitive advantage justifies leaning into the service-area strategy specifically for him.

### Reviews that stick vs. get deleted 33:26

- Reviews are more likely to stick if they come from accounts that are non-local (out of state but still in-country — Google only checks country, not state), and from accounts that have never left a review before and are at least a year old.

### Selling calls to publishers 34:19

- Beyond partnering with contractors, members can monetize excess leads by forwarding calls to pay-per-call publishers (elocal.com, ServiceDirect.com, Ringba.com), which pay per qualified call — e.g. ~$30-50/plumbing call, ~$100/water-damage call — deposited directly, fully passive.
- This lets someone run profiles in markets where they have no local operator at all — they never manage the underlying job, only sell the call.

### Post-construction cleanup niche 37:20

- Randall and Anthony both tried post-construction cleanup and steered away from it — it's a bigger ticket than regular cleaning but comes with recurring "you missed a spot" complaints and bad-review risk that make it not worth it.

### Water damage restoration niche 38:46

- Water damage GMB profiles face different approval odds than plumbing: Google is generally less strict on categories with fewer competing listings (incentivizing coverage), though a water damage profile can still get suspended.
- Art recommends starting with plumbing over water damage, since plumbing issues generate water damage jobs and plumbing teaches the underlying trade; water damage sales rely more on selling the customer than technical expertise, since ~90% of it is paid through insurance rather than the customer directly (insurance typically reimburses the customer, who then pays the contractor). A typical water-damage subcontracting cut is 20-30%.

### Cleaning business economics 42:03

- His VA handles pricing day-to-day using standard formulas based on square footage and deep-clean scope.

### Pricing jobs you don't do yourself 44:51

- For jobs sold but subcontracted out, Art's rule of thumb: call three competitors for quotes, then price in the middle.
- TruSpark's technicians (who came from established local competitors like Anderson, Bill Howe, White Glove) already know market pricing, so he negotiates them down from their default quote rather than setting a fixed price list — pricing stays case-by-case, including after-hours emergency premiums (example: a Sunday-night emergency job billed at $2,100 including a $499 after-hours add-on).

### Lead sources beyond GMB 47:33

- TruSpark tried LSA (Local Services Ads) for plumbing and found it not worth the spend — mostly wasted clicks.
- Art called LSA very competitive for plumbing and suggested PPC, Facebook, and Instagram ads instead; Luke has had good results running Facebook ads for his junk removal business, and Art has been getting leads around $10 each — Facebook ad performance now depends more on ad creative than targeting.

### Top ranking factors 48:38

- Art's ranked list of what drives GMB ranking: (1) market selection (a bad market caps your ceiling regardless of tactics), (2) address vs. no address, (3) listing title (business name + top search term + city), (4) off-page SEO/backlinks, (5) reviews.
- Reviews matter only up to a threshold — Art has sat at ~150 reviews for over a year without needing more, once ~20 reviews ahead of the #2/#3 competitor and holding #1 consistently for 1.5-2 months, ongoing review velocity of 3-5/month is enough to maintain position.

### March 2026 Google core update 51:16

- TruSpark's rankings fluctuated (page 2 to page 8) around a Google-announced March 2026 core update; the group's read (via ChatGPT summaries and a Google Search Central tweet) was that it's a multi-week reshuffle that favors smaller, locally-authentic businesses over large incumbents, seen as a net positive for the group's kind of listings.

### Researching competitor backlinks 52:23

- Art recommends checking top-ranking competitors' backlinks on ahrefs.com (free) and mirroring both the number and type of backlink they have.
- Backlinks vary in difficulty: easy ones (Yelp, Yellow Pages, Nextdoor, Reddit) anyone can create; hard ones (CNN, major news/press coverage) generally can't be replicated on demand.

### Service area radius and zip-code priority 57:06

- Service areas max out at 20 entries per GMB profile; Art fills a city's zip codes first, then stretches another 10-15 miles if needed.
- Randall's priority order for service-area signals: zip codes highest, then neighborhoods, then monuments, then cities, then county — neighborhoods rank above cities because they're more hyper-local, though not every neighborhood name is Google-indexable (worth testing each one; in big cities, school districts can work too).
