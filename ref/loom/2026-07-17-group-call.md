- Raymond recommends researching local competition and income levels before picking a niche; landscaping worked for him because of a high-income service area, but any niche can work if the operator sticks with it.

- Payments run through wire transfer, e-transfer, or card via Stripe integrated into the Jobber CRM; Jobber charges ~2.9% + $0.30 on card payments, which should be passed on to the client via the quote.

- One member has been in plumbing four months making a flat $3-4K/month, blaming low-ticket call volume in his area and difficulty finding contractors who'll take jobs above $400.

- Advice: line up a contractor before the first call so accountability motivates outreach; James reportedly sources all contractors through Indeed's free 3-day job-post trial rather than Facebook Marketplace, regenerating the trial with a new email/card as needed.

### AI truck images and van mismatch concerns 40:28

- AI-generated truck photos on listings haven't caused problems so far; but a mismatch between the van shown and the van that shows up can cost a job, as can location-inconsistent stock photos.

### GMBs per city, lead volume, proximity ranking 42:37

- Eric cautions that 8 GMBs x 100 reviews each in one city would generate more leads than a team could handle; because Google ranks primarily by proximity, then review count, a single well-reviewed address profile can rank for its immediate area without needing to dominate the whole city.

### Verification documents and location spoofing 44:21

- One member verifies by showing business registration and an electricity bill on video; spoofing device location to fake an address got two profiles live before Google started flagging the spoofing pattern.

### Finding addresses across a metro area 46:05

- Eric suggests using storage-unit or co-working locations (indoor or outdoor, either works) spread across neighboring cities (e.g. Vancouver, Burnaby, Surrey) — roughly 15-20 profiles per metro area before hitting diminishing returns.

### SAB video location doesn't need to match listed area 47:45

- For service-area-business videos, Google doesn't cross-reference where the video was filmed against the listed service area, so the same video can support profiles listed anywhere in the same city or country; physical-address profiles still require location spoofing.

### Live video verification via local service ads 48:25

- A contact got GMB approved fast through Google's live-agent video verification by first requesting to run Local Service Ads (which Google is incentivized to approve quickly since it generates ad revenue); the agent only asked for phone number and email, with no location cross-check.

### Buying reviews via Reddit contractor 53:45

- One member pays a contractor in India ~$100/month to source 2-3 reviews per GMB per day by posing as a local in city subreddits; 15 requested so far with no bad reviews.

### GMBs per Gmail account and review pacing 55:02

- Keep to one GMB profile per Gmail account for safety; once a profile is live, reviews can start immediately without waiting.

### Review volume targets for a new address profile 56:15

- Target roughly 50-60 reviews to start on a new address-verified plumbing profile.

### Aged Gmail review stick rate and proxies 56:48

- Buying aged US Gmail accounts for self-reviews works even from non-local emails if paired with a residential proxy (~$5-6 per 5GB, pay-per-data); stick rate runs 60-70%, limited to one review per account per day.

### Google's cross-profile tracking and suspension rules 59:47

- Google detects the same reviewer account leaving reviews across multiple GMBs owned by one operator and can suspend all linked profiles; the safe rule is one review per Gmail account total, spaced at least two weeks apart, max two reviews per account ever — one member had six plumbing profiles suspended this way.

### Recruiting help for energy drink handouts 1:01:47

- Hiring someone else to run the energy-drink review table is harder to staff than doing it personally; younger students avoid approaching people their own age or younger.

### Craigslist and Marketplace for finding help 1:02:54

- Craigslist and Facebook groups work for finding brand-ambassador help (~$14/hr plus commission, or ~$4-5/review); posting with an attached image raises response rate. Doing the table yourself costs ~$0.75/review versus ~$4/review when hiring someone.

### Sourcing and paying plumbing contractors 1:04:42

- Facebook Marketplace/Nextdoor work well for detailing contractors but plumbers are harder to land; Indeed (free 3-day trial) is the most reliable source, per James (~$40K/month); cold-calling low-review GMB listings directly for solo contractors also works, with typical contractor markups around 20-30%.

### Indeed and Craigslist account bans 1:08:20

- Both Indeed employer accounts and Craigslist posting accounts have gotten banned/flagged for verification; the workaround is creating a new account and completing the requested business verification.

### Side jobs and lifestyle tangent 1:08:52

- Several members still hold side jobs despite $4K+/month from their GMB business; the group discusses the demotivation that sets in once a business runs itself, and the importance of saving money against future chargebacks.

### Evaluating niche competition levels 1:12:45

- Cole is reconsidering plumbing after seeing local competitors with 400-1,600+ reviews; Eric's rule of thumb is to target a niche where the top competitor sits under a few hundred reviews, since proximity ranks above review count for address-verified profiles.

### Alternate niches and get-rich-quick warning 1:15:52

- Electrical and fencing are floated as plumbing-adjacent alternatives; Eric warns against chasing same-day-payout niches purely for speed, comparing that mindset to gambling, and notes it breeds laziness.

### Specializing subcontractors by skill 1:17:51

- For multi-service trades like landscaping, Eric recommends a dedicated subcontractor per specialty (turf, retaining walls, etc.) for higher-quality work, though he has since consolidated to one all-around sub.

### How Cole built his subcontractor crew 1:18:16

- Cole started with five subcontractors sourced from Facebook Marketplace, kept them a month while building a legitimate brand reputation, then landed a major crew (former BC Place stadium contractors) through inbound outreach; he stresses meeting subs face-to-face to build trust and staying the one giving out work, not letting subs run the business.

### Website investment and hiring page 1:20:47

- A "join our crew" page was floated but dismissed as unprofessional; Cole instead paid ~$5K for a high-converting website (~30% lift) built by his marketing agency, featuring a team photo.

### Subcontractor payment and tracking structure 1:22:07

- Cole's markup formula: mark up the sub's quote by 15-25%, collect half from the client upfront, pay the sub in full at completion, and keep the remaining markup — the same structure scales from small jobs to $120K contracts.
