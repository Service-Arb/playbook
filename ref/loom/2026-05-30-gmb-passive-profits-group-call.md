Business Profiles verified and for recovering from suspensions. Anmar walks through his storage-unit
signage rig, and the group settles on a business card as the fallback whenever signage fails
verification. Brandon, Chris, and others compare live video verification — now checking vehicle
plates, street signs, and permanence, and increasingly recognizing repeat callers — against
pre-recorded, location-spoofed video, and agree plumbing, locksmiths, and garage-door repair verify

### Call Opens, Roll Call 0:00

- Eric opens the call and hands the floor to Anmar and Vlad to share recent wins.

### Storage-Unit Verification: Signage, Staging, and Backup Business Cards 2:13

- Anmar used a storage unit as his physical address: a printed sign mounted on foam board with
  spacers so it looked mounted rather than flat, plus a foldable desk, monitor, lamp, vacuum, and
  misc. tools staged inside.
- He walked in from across the street, showed nearby businesses, and faked placing an order on his
  laptop; the profile verified the next day.
- Signage is what most people fail on. A business card fixes it for service-area profiles, since
  those don't require a real location — even a plain printed one is enough.

### Electrician vs. Plumbing: Regulatory Risk by Niche 6:04

- Electrician profiles carry higher regulatory risk (Ontario's ESA actively pursues unlicensed
  advertisers); plumbing has no equivalent enforcement body and is more stable to build on.
- Brandon runs four plumbing profiles (Niagara Falls, Winnipeg, Sudbury, Moncton, St. Catharines).

### Brandon's Plumbing Profiles: Live vs. Location-Spoofed Video 8:14

- Brandon's profiles went up through live video verification; only one of ~40 submitted
  location-spoofed pre-recorded videos passed.
- Lives are more reliable for reaching a different area, but getting through the queue without
  getting suspended is the hard part.

### Storage-Unit Address and Unit-Number Requirements 10:27

- Reviewers increasingly reject a bare storage-unit address without showing the unit number on
  camera; one member had to add an office number after too many GMBs shared his address.

### Swapping a Service-Area Listing to a Physical Address 11:55

- Some members have had luck converting a service-area profile to a physical address after the fact;
  it's inconsistent but cheap to try at volume — even a few conversions out of 20-30 pays off.

### Zach's Landscaping Profiles: Suspensions and Phone-Code Risk 12:51

- Zach ran four landscaping profiles without addresses; two got suspended after he verified them
  with phone codes rather than video — phone-code verification is roughly 50/50 and the profiles it
  produces stick less reliably.
- Suspensions traced to phone codes, not to review velocity; reviews only get deleted, not
  suspended.

### Appealing a Suspended Profile 14:35

- To appeal, fake supporting documents, print them, then photograph the printout so Google can't
  read edit metadata from the file.
- Appeals sometimes succeed with no evidence attached at all.

### Landscaping Lead Flow, Markup, and Fulfillment 15:59

- Landscaping jobs vary too much to estimate easily, unlike concrete/flat work; Zach is trying to
  route leads to an established landscaping company and mark up their estimate.
- Typical markup on landscaping jobs is 15-20%, not the 40% common in other niches.

### Suspended High-Volume Profiles vs. Search Volume 19:55

- Zach's two suspended profiles had the highest search volume of his four; the two still-active
  suburb profiles bring in fewer but steadier jobs.

### Shared Gmail Accounts and Suspension Cascade Risk 21:28

- Sharing one Gmail across multiple linked profiles risks all of them getting suspended together if
  one does; a contact who managed ~40 businesses on one account lost all 40 at once.
- New Gmail accounts made on the spot are fine for GMB signup — no need to age them first.

### Live Verification Calls Are Getting Stricter 22:29

- Reps now ask for a company van with branding, a personal vehicle (screenshotting odometer and
  plate for later reference), street signs, and a walkthrough of the business.

### Plumbing Is the Hardest Niche to Verify 24:42

- Plumbing, locksmith, and garage-door repair are the three hardest niches to verify because they're
  considered spammy; landscaping is comparatively easy. Phone-code verification isn't even offered
  for U.S. plumbing profiles.

### Street-Sign and Permanence Pushback on Live Calls 26:00

- Reps are calling out non-permanent signage (e.g. one mounted on a garage door) directly during live
  calls, reasoning it follows a wave of publicized fake-verification videos.

### Anmar's Second Video: Showing Surrounding Businesses 27:17

- After repeated recording failures, Anmar filmed from across the street entirely, using a school's
  concrete address sign and a neighboring business as the surrounding-area shots.

### Spoofing GPS and IP for Verification Videos 28:48

- For pre-recorded (non-live) videos, spoof both GPS coordinates and IP location — live calls don't
  check location unless a rep specifically requests a Google Maps screen share.
- Cellular data IP can resolve to the carrier's routing city rather than the phone's actual location,
  which can work in your favor.
- A $20/month iOS app handles GPS spoofing; IP spoofing comes from switching to cellular data.

### Renting an Office for Live Video Verification 32:27

- An office space (vs. filming from home) helps live-call legitimacy but also exposes more surface
  area — a convincing dashboard and clean staging matter more once a real rep is watching live.

### Printing Realistic Street Signs 34:02

- Street signs run ~$30 on Amazon and are customizable, or can be made from cardboard, an AI-generated
  design, and photo paper glued on — cardboard blows around in wind, which is a known weak point.
- One rejected video cited both missing permanent signage and a missing street sign as reasons.

### Does Google Cross-Reference Street View Against the Video? 37:14

- Speculation that Google may compare submitted video against Street View imagery of the pinned
  location to catch inconsistencies, given real street signs are mounted too high to replicate by
  hand.

### Landscaping Verified With a Bare-Minimum Video 39:10

- One member got a physical-address landscaping profile verified using only a basic service-area
  video of a car — no other staging.

### Finding Fulfillment Contractors via Facebook Groups 40:45

- Facebook's search tab surfaces local "does anyone know a reputable X" posts; commenters offering
  their number are candidate subcontractors — distinguish desperate individuals from people
  advertising their own competing company.
- Contractor-specific Facebook groups exist per city and are a reliable source of subs looking for
  overflow work; calling businesses that already have a GMB profile mostly reaches a gatekeeper.

### Review Strategy: Paying Students per Review 46:28

- One member prints bandit signs near high schools offering $20-30/hr for "hiring," then pays
  students per review with instructions that reviews must come from different people; results have
  stuck reliably at volume (50+ live detailing profiles).

### Junk Removal Income and a Roofing Opportunity 49:12

- A member with junk removal profiles is offered a roofing lead-gen arrangement through a licensed
  GC contact who'll sign off on jobs in exchange for a cut.

### Timing a Service-Area-to-Address Conversion 51:34

- No consistent pattern on how long to let a service-area profile age before converting it to a
  physical address; consensus is not to wait for reviews to accumulate first — convert early.

### Storage-Unit Door Types and Signage Realism 52:36

- Indoor storage units with hallways and interior doors read as more legitimate than outdoor units;
  roll-up garage-style doors are acceptable and match how service businesses like plumbers actually
  load vans.
- A hovering sign (mounted on spacers, or a slide-in acrylic sign frame) reads as more legitimate
  than one flush against a wall; co-working spaces have verified without strict signage scrutiny.

### Closing Advice: Don't Be Lazy, Don't Double-Review 57:08

- The two most common failure points are sloppy signage (crooked, low-effort) and attempting to
  double-review a profile, which gets the review deleted outright.

### Spread Reviews Across Fewer Profiles, Not More 58:28

- Going too wide on profile count while thinning reviews across all of them hurt ranking; consensus
  is to concentrate reviews on fewer profiles instead of scaling profile count.

### Pixel/GrapheneOS Multi-Profile Verification Method 59:15

- Running GrapheneOS on a Google Pixel lets one device host up to 35 separate user profiles, each
  appearing to Google as a distinct device — used with email verification for easier niches, not
  plumbing-tier ones.
- Driving to different blocks avoids Google flagging repeated verifications from the same location.

### Buying Aged Gmail Accounts, and Vetting Sellers 1:00:33

- Aged Gmail accounts (~$3 each) speed up email verification, now working roughly 50% of the time.
- The group calls out a seller ("the Pakistani guy") for reselling already-used Gmail accounts and
  agrees to stop using him; other sources will be shared in the community chat.

### Call Wrap-Up and Next Week 1:05:25

- Eric will post the recording and asks members to post questions in the Skool community instead of
  DMing directly, so answers benefit everyone. Call to reconvene next week.
